The difference is significant:
"This service has never been performed" is a declarative statement of fact that the shop can't actually verify — it's only true relative to what's in the data. A customer who knows they had their coolant flushed at Jiffy Lube three years ago will immediately distrust the entire report.
"No record of this service having been performed" is honest about the data's limitation. It's saying the system doesn't have evidence of it, not that it didn't happen. That's a defensible position and actually opens a better advisor conversation — "we don't have a record of this, do you know if it's been done?" — which invites the customer to share history rather than putting them on the defensive.
It also quietly communicates the value of keeping service records in one place, which is a soft pitch for the customer staying with HEART long-term.
The only small tweak I'd consider: "No record of this service being performed" is slightly cleaner and less formal than "having been performed" — reads more naturally out loud, which matters if advisors are reading from the report with customers.
So the two options side by side:

"No record of this service having been performed." — accurate, slightly formal
"No record of this service being performed." — same meaning, slightly more natural

Either is a meaningful upgrade over the current version. The credibility protection alone makes it worth the change.
