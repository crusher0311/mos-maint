Darren and team,

Here's what our initial review of the elevated Protractor API traffic on August 31 turned up. In short, a sharp spike in inbound callback traffic, including a large share of unknown connections and rapid repeat events, kicked things off, and gaps in our own safeguards amplified part of that into more outbound API calls than should have occurred.

Callback volume

From midnight through roughly 12:10 PM Central, our persisted callback ledger recorded 7,289 accepted callback events:

- 6,109 WorkOrder
- 875 ServiceItem
- 305 Contact
- 6,640 of those were Update operations

Volume climbed sharply around 6:00 AM Central and then held at roughly 1,000–1,600 accepted callbacks per hour.

Those 7,289 accepted callbacks represent only 3,294 unique connection, object, and operation combinations. The rest breaks down as:

- 3,995 repeat deliveries
- 765 repeats arriving within 60 seconds of an identical preceding event
- 292 repeats arriving within one second

**A closer 32-minute sample**

For a roughly 32-minute post-recovery window we have raw logs covering 1,948 inbound callback attempts:

- 545 carried connection IDs that match no active Protractor connection on our side
- 332 were recognized-connection duplicates, skipped before ledger insertion
- 1,071 were accepted and inserted into the ledger

Those three figures reconcile exactly to the 1,948 total.

The 545 unknown-connection callbacks were acknowledged and discarded, and they did not trigger detail requests back to Protractor. Their volume does suggest that stale, unrecognized, or misrouted connection IDs were being delivered to our shared callback endpoint.

The 332 duplicates were caught by our existing deduplication guard. The full ledger shows that other rapid repeats got past it, either because they arrived concurrently, arrived before the preceding event finished processing, or fell outside the guard's short time window.

One caveat worth flagging: unknown callbacks and skipped duplicates never reach our ledger at all, so the true number delivered across the full morning was higher than 7,289.

How that turned into API volume

Accepted WorkOrder and ServiceItem callbacks can prompt our integration to request current object details from Protractor. Our integration did not coalesce concurrent repeat events well enough, and it did not automatically halt outbound requests once Protractor began returning sustained 403 responses. That let the callback activity generate more traffic than necessary and allowed unsuccessful requests to keep going.

So our current assessment is that the callback spike initiated the elevated activity and our safeguard gaps amplified it. That said, 7,289 persisted callback records do not reconcile with the roughly 250,000 API calls you reported, so we need to compare records before reaching a final conclusion about the complete traffic source.

The incident is contained.
